How the app currently works

The app runs on your computer at localhost:3000. A Chrome extension connects it to a Google Photos tab where you are already signed in. You do not need an OAuth client ID or a Google Cloud project.

1. LoadRead 50 photos and videos from Google Photos.
2. SwipeKeep an item or queue it for removal.
3. SendAdd queued items to a private review album.
4. Delete manuallyReview the album and move selected items to Trash in Google Photos.

A left swipe does not delete a photo. This build has no deletion command. Sending the queue adds items to an album; it does not free storage. You perform the deletion in Google Photos.

The three parts

PartWhat it does
Web appDisplays previews, plays videos, shows review progress, and lets you keep, queue, or undo a decision.
Local backendStores imported metadata, decisions, authentication sessions, and album-update checkpoints in SQLite on your computer.
Chrome extensionUses the signed-in Google Photos tab to read library items and add candidates to an album. Google login cookies and page authentication tokens stay in Chrome.

This connection uses Google Photos’ undocumented web API, adapted from Google Photos Toolkit. Google can change that API, so the connection may need maintenance.

Set it up yourself

You need Chrome, access to your Google Photos account, this project folder, and Node.js 22.13 or newer. There are no npm dependencies to install and no hosted services to pay for in this local version.

  1. Start the local server.

    In Terminal, run:

    cd /Users/siddharthlakkoju/Documents/ChatGPT/GooglePhotos
node --version
npm start

    Leave that Terminal process running. If the project is in another location, use that folder instead. Keep the default port, 3000.

  2. Sign in to the local app in Chrome.

    The first start generates a private access code. The access link is saved in .data/local-login-url. On this Mac, you can open it directly in Chrome:

    open -a "Google Chrome" "$(cat .data/local-login-url)"

    Alternatively, open http://localhost:3000/ in Chrome and enter the code from .data/access-code. The local sign-in session lasts 12 hours. Do not share either file.

  3. Load the extension.

    Open chrome://extensions in Chrome. Enable Developer mode, click Load unpacked, and choose:

    /Users/siddharthlakkoju/Documents/ChatGPT/GooglePhotos/extension

    Refresh the app tab after installation. Pin Photos Swipe — Desktop Bridge to the Chrome toolbar if you want quicker access.

  4. Generate a pairing code.

    In the app, click Google Photos → Generate pairing code. Each code expires after five minutes and can be used once.

  5. Pair the correct Google account.

    Open Google Photos in another Chrome tab and sign in. While that tab is active, open the Photos Swipe extension popup, enter the code, and click Pair this Google account.

  6. Load your library.

    Return to the app, click Check connection in the Google Photos dialog. The button shows Checking…, then the dialog reports the verified account and time. If local sign-in expired, sign in again and retry. Close the dialog after verification. Click Load 50 more. Your account email appears in the sidebar.

Use Chrome for the working app. The Codex in-app browser cannot use this Chrome extension. Use exactly http://localhost:3000/, rather than 127.0.0.1 or a different port.

Send candidates to the review album

  1. Open Removal queue and check your choices.
  2. Click Send to review album and confirm the candidate count.
  3. Keep the app and paired Google Photos tabs open while the update runs.
  4. Wait for the verified count, then click Open review album.

The app verifies membership using Google’s stable content identifiers, because album media IDs can differ from library IDs. If an update is interrupted, Retry and verify checks existing membership before adding missing items. If the saved album no longer exists, the updated extension finds or creates a replacement album and verifies your preserved selections there.

Choosing Keep instead after an item has been sent does not remove it from the review album. Remove it from the album manually before using that album as a deletion batch.

Delete the album’s photos in Google Photos

  1. Open the review album in Google Photos on desktop.
  2. Select the first item with its checkmark. Hold Shift and click the last item to select the range.
  3. Choose ⋮ More options → Move to trash, then confirm.

Remove from album and deleting the album itself leave the underlying photos in your library. Use Move to trash to delete the photos. See Google’s deletion guide.

After deleting in Google Photos, open Removal queue → Check deleted items. This read-only check scans Trash and verifies album membership. If the old review album is unavailable, confirmed Trash matches still apply and the app clears the broken album link for the next batch. Confirmed Trash items leave the active queue and review stack; their decision history is retained locally. Items removed from the album can be sent again after their state is verified. Failed or unknown checks do not discard an item. Restoring an item and loading its library page brings it back with its saved decision. If an item was permanently purged before a confirmed Trash check, absence alone cannot prove deletion; an unknown result is left unchanged.

What needs to stay running

Keep the local server running while using the app. The paired Google Photos tab is needed for loading new batches, uncached previews, video playback, and album updates. Cached previews can be reviewed while the bridge is disconnected, as long as the local server is running.

If the paired Google Photos tab closes, open the same account in another Photos tab and check the connection. Bridge 1.2.0 can recover that tab after verifying its account. A live paired tab that changes accounts is never silently replaced. After restarting the server, your saved library, decisions and crop jobs remain; open the app and check the connection again.

Crop a video segment

Current status: the editor, actual exported clip, browser-session upload, capture metadata and decoded playback have been verified end to end in Chrome with Bridge 1.2.0, including mobile viewport checks for the editor. Approving a crop immediately queues the exact original and advances review while its replacement uploads. That original is marked pending and excluded from album sending until verification; the cropped copy stays out of review. The optional official API adapter remains available but has not been tested with a live grant.

  1. While reviewing a video, press C or choose Crop video. C also works in enlarged review.
  2. Drag the yellow start/end boundaries. The preview follows the moved boundary; playback loops inside the selection. Use the scrub control independently. Arrow keys move a focused handle one thirtieth of a second; Shift changes the step to 0.1 seconds.
  3. Choose Cancel to restore the prior playback position, or Approve crop to save the job.
  4. Approval starts preparing and uploading the crop. Keep the local app and signed-in Google Photos tab open; the job shows progress below the review. If the stored-source download is blocked, use Google Photos’ ordinary download and select that stored original file in the job.
  5. If a job stops, its saved export and upload checkpoint remain available. Use Preview exported clip to inspect the export and Resume upload / verification to continue. Approval already queued the original; verified success makes it eligible for Send to review album.

The source is never deleted. Cropped copies remain kept and excluded across restart/import. Open the replacement link or choose Review this generated clip later after verification to bring it into review. Undo restores the original’s prior decision and keeps the replacement.

On Approve, the original immediately leaves review and enters the unsent queue as Pending replacement verification. Upload starts eagerly. You can send other eligible candidates while this source waits. Failed work stays queued with Retry; Cancel or safe Undo restores its previous decision. No original is deleted.

Upload using your Google Photos tab

The default browser-session provider uses the same paired Chrome account as review. Google cookies and upload-session credentials stay in Chrome. Crops have persistent jobs and unique filenames so interrupted creation can be reconciled without blindly uploading another copy. Reload the updated desktop bridge once after updating its files.

Optional official upload setup

To use the optional official API fallback, create a Google Cloud project, enable Photos Library API, configure its consent screen/test user, and create a web OAuth client. Register http://localhost:3000/api/trim-oauth/callback as the redirect URI. In Video crop setup, choose that client JSON and then Connect official upload API. You must select the same Google account as the paired Chrome tab and approve the provider’s consent screen yourself.

The requested access is identity/email for account binding, uploading new media, and reading app-created media. This does not grant broad library reads. OAuth tokens are saved privately on this Mac; the existing Google session stays in Chrome. No credentials or account consent were supplied by the scheduled run. Official API uploads use original quality and may count toward your storage.

Recovery and verification

Jobs retain confirmed export/upload progress across restarts. Resume verifies the upload offset or reconciles an uncertain creation before trying another write. Time/place, decoded playback, processing readiness, duration, dimensions and audio must match before the original changes. The earlier Google Photos upload experiment lost its capture time, so embedded metadata alone is insufficient proof; any remote mismatch leaves the original unchanged.

For a preview/stored-duration mismatch, the job offers the precise stored end time for explicit acceptance. Cancel is available before uploading. After the exact replacement is known, Abandon crop · leave both copies frees the job while preserving the original and excluding the generated copy. Ambiguous uploads require reconciliation first. Closed jobs can clean their local recovery files; successful scratch files expire after a day while the app runs.

Export requires macOS/Xcode, limits sources to 1 GB, and requires 2 GB free disk. Passthrough preserves encoded media when its verification checks pass. Unsupported formats fail rather than silently degrading quality. HDR sources remain unverified. The editor was checked at 320×568, 390×844, 430×932 and 844×390; short screens scroll the dialog. Actual iOS/Safari touch behavior still needs testing.

Troubleshooting and local data

The page will not load

Check that npm start is still running. Use http://localhost:3000/. If the Terminal reports that the port is already in use, an existing server may already be running; check it before starting another one.

The extension is missing or disconnected

Use Chrome, make sure the extension is enabled, and refresh the app tab. Click Google Photos → Check connection. If the paired tab closed, open the same account in Google Photos and check again. If the account changed, switch back or explicitly pair the correct tab. A missing extension response asks you to reload the extension and this app tab; a connected older bridge can still import but displays an update notice for video crops.

I changed the extension code

In chrome://extensions, click the circular Reload icon inside the Photos Swipe — Desktop Bridge card, then refresh the app tab. Refreshing browser tabs alone does not reload installed extension code. Ordinary HTML/CSS/frontend changes require only refreshing the app tab; server changes require restarting npm start.

Check connection seems unchanged, or pairing fails

The dialog now shows progress, followed by a fresh verification time or an error. A local sign-in screen means your app session expired; sign in before checking again. Pairing codes expire after five minutes and work once. Generate a fresh code, open the extension popup on the signed-in Google Photos tab, enter the code, then return here and check. An incorrect or expired code leaves an existing pairing intact. If video crops say the bridge needs updating, reload the project extension and this app tab once; its permissions are unchanged.

Chrome blocks the original download

If Chrome shows ERR_BLOCKED_BY_CLIENT for Google’s download host, source retrieval cannot finish. The saved original stays queued and marked pending; it cannot be sent to the review album yet. Try the exact original’s ordinary Google Photos download yourself, then choose that original file in the saved job. If the normal download is also blocked, browser access must be restored before retrying. The app does not bypass Chrome security blocks.

Google is still preparing the stored original

Bridge 1.2.2 checks the same acknowledged download request. If preparation exceeds the bounded wait, choose Resume; it reuses that request and preserves the crop. When Google packages the original in ZIP, the app automatically extracts the exact matching movie after file-size, CRC and safe archive checks, then performs the usual source/export validation. Unexpected or ambiguous archive contents stop safely with a visible error. A validated extracted source resumes locally without downloading again.

A preview or video will not load

Keep the paired Photos tab open and signed in. Try Refresh latest for recent items, then retry the preview. Videos need a live connection; if playback fails, use the Google Photos link when offered. Private API changes may require a provider update.

An album update was interrupted

Restore the connection and choose Retry and verify. The app keeps confirmed progress. Do not change candidates while an update is running. If you manually remove items from the album, the app’s saved acknowledgement may no longer reflect that album.

Where my data is stored

.data/photos.sqlite stores metadata, decisions, hashed credentials, and job checkpoints. .data/access-code and .data/local-login-url provide local sign-in. These files are ignored by Git. Chrome’s IndexedDB stores cached preview images separately; signing out does not erase them. Do not publish or share .data/.

Disconnect or use a different account

Revoke extension pairing in the app invalidates its credential. Forget pairing in the popup removes the browser’s copy. Remove the extension through Chrome when finished. A workspace is bound to one Google account; use a separate workspace for another account.
